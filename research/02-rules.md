# 02 - Rules of La Belle Lucie (primary-source comparison)

Scope: what period and mid-century books actually say. All quotes are [OCR] from the saved text copies unless noted. Local copies are in `research/sources/02/`. Source list at the bottom.

## 1. Sources read (summary)

- Cadogan, *Illustrated Games of Patience* (S1, S2): the earliest English text I could read. Gives a one-pack La Belle Lucie.
- Dick's *Games of Patience* (S3): reprints Cadogan's text with an extra rule and an extra clause on the last redeal. It also prints Shamrocks.
- Cavendish, *Patience Games* (S4, De La Rue 1890): calls the same game "The Fan" and has different rules.
- Hoffmann, *Illustrated Book of Patience Games* (S5, 1892): "The Trefoil". Same family, with the aces pre-removed.
- French texts (S6 1876, S7 1923, S8 undated, IA says 1880): the French "La Belle Lucie" is played with TWO packs.
- Morehead & Mott-Smith (S9, 1949): the modern standard, with named aliases.
- Not obtained: Bergholt, Dalton, Parlett, Jones Series I (Jones Series II lists a "Fair Lucy" that I could not read; see Q10), and any German, Italian or Russian text. IA lending copies expose no full text. I did not cite Wikipedia for any fact (the saved raw page is a pointer only).

## 2. Clause-by-clause comparison

Abbreviations: "silent" means the source does not say. "suit" means in suit.

| Clause | Cadogan S1/S2 | Dick's S3 | Cavendish "The Fan" S4 | Morehead S9 | French S6 (1876) | French S7 (1923) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Packs | 1 (52) [S1] | 1 [S3] | 1 [S4] | 1 [S9] | 2 (104) [S6] | 2 (104) [S7] |
| Layout | Packets of 3, last packet 1 card (i.e. 17 x 3 + 1) [S1] | same [S3] | 17 depots of 3, "one card over ... a depot to itself" [S4] | 17 fans of 3, leftover card a separate pile [S9] | Packets of 3 to exhaustion, last packet of 2; rows of 6 or 7 [S6] | 34 groups of 3 plus a 35th of 2; 6 or 7 per row [S7] |
| Foundations | 4 aces, build up in suit to K [S1] | same [S3] | same; aces taken when on top [S4] | same; aces moved as they become available [S9] | Aces set above the first row; 2, 3 ... to K in suit [S6] | 8 aces, up to K, same suit [S7] |
| Tableau building | "marriages in a descending line"; suit not stated in S1/S2 [S1] | Rule III: "Marriages must be formed in suit" [S3] | Descending, in suit [S4] | "build down in suit" [S9] | "en ligne descendante, et toujours de la même famille" (down, always same suit) [S6] | Same suit, one rank above or below; "certains joueurs" also allow alternating colours [S7] |

| Last-chance "draw" | silent in S1/S2 | "In the last deal it is permitted to draw one card if it should happen to be covered with another card that would otherwise defeat the Patience" [S3] | not for The Fan | "After the last redeal, any one buried card may be drawn out of any one fan" [S9] | silent | silent |
| Win | silent explicitly; implied by foundations to K [S1] | same | All 4 sequences built [S4] | all cards to foundations (implied by layout) [S9] | "souches commencées par les as sont fermées par les rois" (suits started by aces closed by kings) [S6] | 8 hierarchies of 13 complete [S7] |
| Kings | silent | silent | King may go to a space [S4] | Not specified for La Belle Lucie (tips discuss kings burying cards) [S9] | First kings dealt on the packets cannot be touched until the end; a king may temporarily go on a same-suit queen [S6] | silent |

Verbatim anchors (OCR):

- S1 Rule I: "The uppermost card of each packet is alone available, until by its removal it releases the one beneath." Rule II: "The foundations must follow suit."
- S1 layout: "Deal out the entire pack in packets of three cards dealt together and placed as in tableau. The last packet, however, will contain but one card."
- S3 adds: "III. Marriages must be formed in suit."
- S4 spaces: "When a space is left, by taking or moving all the cards from a depot, any king on the top of a depot may be moved to the space".
- S6 kings: "l'on ne peut toucher aux premiers rois qui sont sur les paquets des trois cartes primitivement placées" (one cannot touch the kings that sit on the three-card packets as first dealt).
- S6 unlimited building: "L'on peut ... poser autant de cartes que l'on veut sur les paquets en ligne descendante, et toujours de la même famille" (one may place as many cards as one wishes on the packets in descending line, always of the same suit).
- S7 deals: "si, au bout de 3 distributions seulement, vous n'avez pas réussi ... la patience est manquée" (if after only 3 deals you have not completed the families, the patience is lost). S7 also: "ramassez ce qui reste sur le tableau sans toucher aux paquets des hiérarchies" (pick up what is left on the tableau without touching the foundation piles).

## 3. Named variants as the sources define them

| Name | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The Fan | Cavendish's name for the one-pack game: 17 depots + 1 single, build down in suit, king to space allowed, one deal. Variation: no king to spaces, 3 deals. | [S4] |
| Fan / Clover Leaf / Midnight Oil / Alexander the Great | Listed as aliases of La Belle Lucie. | [S9] |
| Trefoil | Aces removed beforehand and put in the foundation row; 16 fans of 3 (48 cards). Hoffmann: tableau top cards only; you gather the triplets WITHOUT shuffling and re-lay; "permitted to deal through the stock three times" (2 redeals). Making aces emerge in play is offered as a harder option. Two packs: 8 aces, 32 triplets, one pass only. | [S5], [S9] |
| Les Fleurons | French rendering of the same Trefoil text (16 packets of 3, "trois fois", unshuffled). | [S8] |
| Shamrocks / Three-card Fan | Cavendish's Three-Card Fan: kings placed at the bottom of their depot when dealing; top cards may be moved between depots regardless of suit; no depot over 3 cards; one deal. Dick's: marriages need not be in suit, max 3 per packet, "if a king should cover any card ... of the same suit" it may go underneath, no redeal. Morehead: build up or down regardless of suit, max 3 per fan, king over a same-suit card moves below it, spaces never filled, draw out a buried ace if blocked. | [S3], [S4], [S9] |
| House in the Wood (Cadogan, Dick's) | Two packs, 8 aces as foundations, double the packets. Otherwise La Belle Lucie. | [S1], [S3] |
| House in the Wood (Cavendish "Double Fan") | Two packs, 4 ascending (aces) + 4 descending (kings) foundations, in suit; build either way in suit; aces/kings not moved to spaces or put at bottom; one deal. | [S4] |
| House in the Wood (Morehead) | Two decks, 34 fans of 3 + 1 of 2; build up OR down in suit on the tableau, no redeal. | [S9] |
| House on the Hill | Two packs; 4 aces + 4 kings (different suits) as foundations; marriages built both ascending and descending. (Morehead: aces up, kings down.) | [S1], [S3], [S9] |
| Double Three-Card Fan | Two packs; Three-Card Fan rules but in suit, no king moves; a single "merci" (put a king over a same-suit card at the bottom); "Some players take a second merci". | [S4] |
| La Belle Lucie (French, 2 packs) | The French books play with 104 cards, not 52. | [S6], [S7] |

Names NOT found in any primary text I read: "Alexandra", "Good Measure", "Three Shuffles and a Draw", "La Belle Benedikte". They appear only in Wikipedia's article (pointer, not cited), which I could not trace to a source. The mechanic that Wikipedia calls "a draw" does exist in S3 and S9 (one buried card drawn after the last deal), and Cavendish calls a related relief "merci" [S4]. Whether the name "Three Shuffles and a Draw" is the older term is unverified.

## 4. Tally and proposed canonical ruleset

Tally (one-pack English sources S1-S4, S9 = 5 sources; French sources noted separately):

- 17 fans of 3 + 1 single: 5 of 5 (S1, S2/S3 via copy, S4, S9). Cavendish and Morehead call the leftover a "depot to itself" / "separate pile" [S4][S9].
- Aces to four foundations, build up in suit to K: 5 of 5.
- Tableau building down in suit: explicit in S3, S4, S9 (3 of 5); S1/S2 silent; both French sources agree (S6, S7). No source gives alternating colour as the main rule (S7: "certains joueurs" only).
- Only exposed (top) cards move: 5 of 5.
- Two redeals with shuffle: S1, S3, S9 (3 of 5); S4 says 1 deal (or 3 with the variation); S7 3 deals. Modal answer: 3 deals total (2 redeals), gather non-foundation cards, shuffle, deal again in 3s, remainder as a short fan.
- Spaces: S9 never filled, S4 filled by king only; S1-S3 silent. Majority of explicit statements go both ways (1 each), so this is the weakest point.
- Last-chance draw of one buried card after the last deal: S3, S9 (2 of 5; absent from S1 and S4).

Proposed canonical ruleset (default "Classic", following the majority, and flagged where it rests on one source):

1. One 52-card pack. Deal all cards face up in 17 fans of 3, the last card forming a fan of 1. [S1][S4][S9]
2. Foundations: aces, built up in suit to king. An ace on top of a fan may go straight up. [S1][S9]
3. Tableau: top card of a fan may be moved onto the top card of another fan if the same suit and one rank lower. One card at a time. [S3][S4][S9]
4. Only a top card is playable; removing it releases the next. [S1]
5. Empty fans stay empty and are never refilled. (S9; S1-S3 silent; S4 differs.)
6. Redeals: when stuck, the player may collect all non-foundation cards, shuffle, and deal again in fans of 3 with the remainder as a smaller fan. Two redeals at most. [S1][S9]
7. After the last redeal, once only, the player may draw one buried card from any fan and play it. [S3][S9] (S3's wording limits it to a card covered by one that "would otherwise defeat the Patience"; S9 allows any buried card.)
8. Win: all 52 cards on the foundations. [S1]

Option toggles, each sourced:
- Trefoil: aces pre-placed, 16 fans of 3. [S5][S9]
- The Fan (easy): king may go to an empty fan; no redeals. [S4]
- Redeal without shuffling (collect in order). [S3][S5]
- Alternating-colour building. Marked as a house variation only. [S7]
- Shamrocks: max 3 per fan, any suit, king-below deal rule, no redeal. [S3][S9]
- Two-pack versions: House in the Wood / House on the Hill. [S1][S3][S4][S9]

## 5. Open questions the sources leave open

1. Did the original (Cadogan) require build-in-suit? S1/S2 do not say; S3 adds it as Rule III. Is the 1884 Dick's text from a later Cadogan edition? I did not check this.
2. Can a built-up run be moved as a unit? S9 says "one card at a time". S1-S4 are silent.
3. May a card be moved onto a fan of 1? Obviously yes by S9 usage; no source states it explicitly.
4. The empty-fan rule: S9 says never refilled, S4 says a king may fill it, S1-S3 are silent. The "king rule" is Cavendish's.
5. What precisely happens to kings in the standard game? S6 says the original kings cannot be touched; S1-S3 and S9 are silent on any special treatment.
6. Is "one buried card" any card (S9) or a card covered by a would-be defeater (S3)? Is the draw once per game or once per deal? S4 "some players take a second merci".
7. When exactly may a redeal be called? S1 says "when all available cards have been played". Is a redeal compulsory or optional if moves remain? Silent.
8. The number of fans after a redeal: S9 says remainder of 1 or 2 forms a short fan; S1-S3 are silent. Is the order of cards in redeal specified? S3 reports variants.
9. Why do the French books use two packs? I did not find the origin. The French text of S6 appears to describe a different (two-pack) game under the same name; this is a possible translation or naming drift. Cadogan's House in the Wood is the English counterpart.
10. Jones Series I (which lists "Fair Lucy") and Bergholt, Dalton, Parlett were not read. Whether "Fair Lucy" is La Belle Lucie is [UNVERIFIED]. German, Italian and Russian texts were not found.
11. Date of Cadogan's first edition: Wikipedia says 1870 and a facsimile of 1875 (pointer only). I have not confirmed this. The Gutenberg and IA copies I read (S1, S2) postdate the Duke of Albany's death (March 1884), since the dedication says "his late Royal Highness".
